Take a page apart

This is a real teacher website, not a diagram. Press any part of it.

A working page, six parts every page you will ever build has these
abirwafa.pages.dev

The order is fixed. A visitor decides in about three seconds, so the opening screen comes second and the footer comes last.

1 · Navigation bar. The signs above the doors in a school corridor. Same place, same order, on every page.

Five or six names. Seven and the visitor stops reading.

2 · Opening screen. The front cover of a book: one big line, one short line, one button.

The big line says who you are and what you do for the reader. “Welcome to my website” says neither.

3 · Panels. Lunch trays: identical shape, one meal each. One panel per course, per resource, per service.

Two panels that look different tell the visitor they mean different things.

4 · Gallery. The display board in the corridor. This is the part that actually persuades.

No learner's face without written permission from the family. When in doubt, photograph the work.

5 · Buttons. Doorbells. One loud one per screen; everything else is quieter.

Write what will happen: “Book a training day”, not “Click here”.

6 · Footer. The note by the front door with the phone number on it. Email, WhatsApp, city, year.

It is on every page, so a private link here is a leak everywhere.

Glued to the top edge, full width, and it does not move while the content slides underneath it. The safest of the three: it is what most sites do, so nobody has to learn it. In the builder: Sticky Minimal

3 · Split header Count the boxes: two, not one.
Abir Wafa Home Courses Contact

The logo sits in its own box on one side and the names in another, with a gap between them. The gap is what makes a real logo look deliberate. If your logo is only your name in text, use one of the other two. In the builder: Split Header

On a phone all three become the same thing: a logo and three stacked lines that open the names as a list. Ask for that in your prompt.

Whichever you choose: the same bar on every page, same position, names in the same order, and it shows which page the visitor is on.

Decide your pages

Six pages cover almost every teacher. Write this list before anything else.

index.html
Home One screen. Opening line, three panels, one button. Its only job is a second page.
about.html
About me Photograph, qualifications, years, places. Write it as “I”, not as “Dr Abir”.
courses.html
Courses One panel per course: who it is for, the CEFR level, the hours, what they can do at the end.
resources.html
Resources The worksheets and videos they download. The page that brings people back, and the one most likely to leak something private.
gallery.html
Gallery Twelve strong pictures beat sixty ordinary ones. Each openable at full size.
contact.html
Contact Email, a WhatsApp button, three form fields. Say how fast you reply.

Start with one long page. The names scroll the visitor down instead of loading a new page. Splitting it later is easy; merging five thin pages after you have given out the links is not.

Choose the gallery

The same six pictures, hung four ways.

Live galleries the arrows and the enlarging both work

Before you upload any picture: shrink it to about 1600 pixels wide, write one line describing it, and check you have permission to publish it.

Even grid. Every picture cropped to the same shape. Tidy, and it never breaks. The cost is cut edges, so keep faces away from the border.

Brick wall. Nothing cropped: each picture keeps its own height and they stack with no gaps. The honest choice when you did not plan the photographs.

One at a time. Most visitors never press the arrow, so whatever is on picture two is not really on your website. Use three or four, strongest first.

Press to enlarge. Not a fourth gallery: a behaviour you add on top. Press any picture above and it opens full size. Its name is a lightbox; say that word in your prompt.

Choose the buttons

Press one. Nothing goes anywhere, but the line underneath says what a visitor would expect.

Six live buttons one loud one per screen, never six

Four rules. Write what will happen, not “Click here”. One loud button per screen. At least 44 pixels tall so a thumb can hit it. And it must look different while it is being pressed.

Build the contact form

That table is the whole point of SCORM. You wrap the lesson in an agreed shape, so Moodle, Blackboard, Canvas or your school's own system can open it and keep the record.

When you need it: when marks must be tracked. Not for a public link, which is what this session builds. Its newer relative, xAPI, records far more detail.

The trap that catches everybody once. A SCORM package will not open by double-clicking. It needs a learning platform to talk to, which is why a colleague sends you one and says it is broken.

Publish it free

cloudflare.com/drop

Drag your folder in, get a live link. No sign-up, no settings. Perfect for a training room: every teacher sees their own site on the internet inside a minute.

The link lasts about an hour. Inside that hour you can sign in and claim it, and it becomes permanent. Do not hand a Drop link to a parent.

Route B · the permanent free site

  1. Put the site in one folder

    Name the main page exactly index.html, small letters, top of the folder.

    Watch out: one level too deep and the page opens blank. This causes almost every “my site is empty”.

  2. Create a free Cloudflare account

    dash.cloudflare.com/sign-up, work email, confirm from the message they send.

    Watch out: no card needed, and do not move your school's existing domain into Cloudflare to do this.

  3. Workers & Pages, then Create application

    Then Get started, then Drag and drop your files.

    Watch out: Cloudflare renames these buttons often. Look for “drag and drop” or “direct upload”.

  4. Name the project: this becomes your address

    https://abir-wafa-teaching.pages.dev

    Watch out: changing the name later means a new link, so every address you gave out stops working. No spaces, no capitals, no Arabic letters.

  5. Drag the folder in, press Deploy site

    Drag the whole folder, not file by file, so the pictures keep their places.

    Watch out: up to 1,000 files, 25 MB each. One refused file means uploading the whole folder again, so compress your pictures first.

  6. Test it on mobile data, not school wifi

    Check the pages, pictures, the WhatsApp button and one download.

    Watch out: school networks hold old copies and block new addresses. You would spend an afternoon fixing nothing.

  7. To change it later

    Open the project, Create a new deployment, drag the folder in again. The link never changes.

    Watch out: keep the folder on your own computer as the real version. What is on Cloudflare is a copy.

  8. To put your own domain on it

    In the project: Custom domains, Set up a domain, type your address, copy its lines into your registrar. The padlock is free.

    Watch out: an address change takes hours to reach everybody. Do not announce it until you have seen it work on mobile data.

The free plan: 20,000 files per site, 25 MB per file, 500 publishes a month, 100 of your own domains. A teacher website is under fifty files. Cloudflare raises these, so check before promising your school anything.

Before you press publish. A published site is readable by the whole world. Nothing on it identifies a learner: no full name, no mark, no photograph without written permission.
